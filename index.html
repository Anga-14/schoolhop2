Digital Marketing Plan

Landing Page Copy

SchoolHop Foundation helps thousands of rural learners reach school safely every day. By sponsoring transport routes, you help improve attendance rates, educational success, and future opportunities for children across the Eastern Cape.

SMART Campaign Objective

Increase completed sponsorship applications by 30% within six months, compared with the previous six-month baseline, through search advertising, community engagement, social media, and donor partnerships. Review qualified enquiries and application completion weekly.

Target Audience Profile

Primary audience: South African businesses and CSR/ESG decision-makers seeking credible, locally relevant education and community investment opportunities. They need transparent route costs, measurable outcomes, partner due diligence, and clear reporting.

Secondary audiences: individual donors and community supporters who want an understandable way to help; educators and local organisations who can refer communities, validate needs, and coordinate routes. Prioritise Eastern Cape relevance, accessible language, mobile-first content, and clear contact options.

Customer Persona

Nomsa, Corporate Social Investment Manager

Profile: A time-poor decision-maker at a South African business, evaluating education partnerships with evidence of community need and accountable outcomes.

Goals: Identify a practical, credible partnership; understand funding options; report outcomes internally.

Pain points: Vague impact claims, unclear costs, slow follow-up, and difficulty comparing opportunities.

Decision needs: Route/learner sponsorship options, a named contact, transparent follow-up, and a plan for impact reporting.

Best message and action: “Explore a route partnership” linking to sponsorship choices and a short enquiry form.

Digital-Marketing Funnel

  1. Awareness: Search ads, organic search, and social stories introduce the safe-transport challenge.
  2. Consideration: Landing page explains the service, community impact, sponsorship options, and evidence.
  3. Intent: Visitor selects a sponsorship option or requests partnership information.
  4. Conversion: Validated sponsorship application or qualified enquiry is submitted.
  5. Nurture: With explicit consent, send a welcome email and relevant impact updates; report partner outcomes.

SEO Keyword & On-Page Plan

  • High intent: “sponsor a learner South Africa”, “school transport sponsorship”, “sponsor a school transport route”. Use on sponsorship headings, form copy, and campaign landing page.
  • Local/service: “Eastern Cape rural school transport”, “safe learner transport Eastern Cape”. Use in service and location-relevant copy.
  • Partner intent: “education CSI projects South Africa”, “corporate social investment education”. Use in partnership content and LinkedIn materials.
  • Keep one descriptive page title and meta description, use a clear heading hierarchy, descriptive image alt text, meaningful internal links, fast mobile layouts, and useful original copy.
  • Before launch, verify page indexing, canonical URL, social share metadata, accessibility, image performance, and Search Console ownership on the real domain. Avoid keyword stuffing; validate search demand with live keyword tools.

Campaign results interpretation

At the sample baseline of 1,240 delivered emails, 38% open rate implies about 471 opens, 6.4% click-through implies about 79 clicks, and 2.1% conversion implies about 26 applications. The rates exceed the prototype review thresholds of 25%, 4%, and 2%, respectively. These are rounded sample figures—not observed or verified campaign results.

Optimisation: preserve the working message as a control, test segment-specific subject lines and landing-page copy, simplify sponsorship form completion, and track qualified applications and unsubscribes. Compare against the previous six-month baseline and wait for adequate sample size before declaring a winner.
